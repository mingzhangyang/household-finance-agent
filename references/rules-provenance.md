**Independent verifier copy.** `scripts/verify_outputs.py` retypes the 2026 values above (brackets, standard and age-65 deductions, gain zones, Social Security bases, enhanced senior deduction, FICA caps and rates, Additional Medicare thresholds, base elective limit, the $3,000 capital loss limit) instead of importing `tax2026.py`. It also retypes the Uniform Lifetime Table and checks every RMD divisor against it. When a register value changes, change the engine and the verifier and record the source check; a one-sided change fails verification.

## Published tax years and labeled projections — not law

Indexed amounts are kept per tax year, separately from projections, in `tax2026.PUBLISHED_*` (engine) and a retyped copy in `verify_outputs.py` (verifier):

| Rule family | Published years | Source IDs | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| `income_tax_tables` | 2026 | `brackets_standard_senior` | Ordinary brackets, standard deduction, age-65 addition, 0%/15% gain zones |
| `oasdi_wage_base` | 2026, 2027 | `payroll`, `payroll_2027` | Social Security wage base per person |
| `elective_deferral_limit` | 2026 | `401k_elective_limit_2026` | Base §402(g) limit; catch-ups are rejected |

For each family and year: a published year uses its own values (`published`, multiplier 1). A later year multiplies the **latest published year's** values by `(1+future_bracket_inflation)^(year-base_year)` and is labeled `projected`. Earlier or skipped years are rejected. This is a **user assumption**, not a prediction of IRS guidance: published amounts are rounded and index from different base years (see the [primary-document review](primary-source-review-2026-10-29.md)). Every row carries `rule_basis` (status, base year, multiplier, source ID per family) and `tax.policy`, which names the published year and, for a projection, says it is NOT published law. The report's year audit shows the same labels.

Fixed statutory amounts are not indexed and are not projections: Publication 915 base amounts, NIIT and Additional Medicare thresholds, payroll rates, the $3,000 capital loss limit, and the $6,000 enhanced senior deduction with its 6% phaseout, which the statute limits to 2025–2028. Projected years assume current statute continues.

**Adding a newly published year** (for example the 2027 revenue procedure): read the official document, add the year to the engine registry, the verifier's retyped copy and the report template's published-year list (`publishedRuleYears`) with the same source ID, record the check here, and add a golden case from its printed tables. That year becomes `published`, and later years rebase on it, so results change: ship it as its own PR and regenerate reports. A change to only one copy fails verification or the template test (`tests/test_rule_years.py`).

Thresholds (exemption steps, senior credit, surtax, Frederick bands) compare the cent-rounded FAGI or taxable net income that the row exports, so the verifier reproduces the engine's choice exactly. Maryland tax is not deducted on Form 8960 line 9b because the federal return takes the standard deduction (IRC §63(e); 26 CFR 1.1411-4(f)(3)(iii)); **flag for professional review**. When a recognized loss lowers FAGI across one of these thresholds while funding a year, the Maryland tax can fall by more than the extra sale, so no draw pays exactly its own tax; the engine then stops at the least draw that pays everything and leaves the excess in cash (`funding_cliff_excess_cash`), which the verifier accepts only if one more cent of FAGI raises its own recomputed Maryland tax by at least that excess.

Salary deferrals are exempt from modeled ordinary federal income tax but NOT Social Security/Medicare payroll tax. Traditional IRA/401(k) RMDs and additional withdrawals are modeled as ordinary income. The Roth conversion is from fully pretax IRA to qualified Roth IRA belonging to the same person; no tax-free nondeductible IRA basis. IRS distributions, penalties and early RMD deferrals outside these cases are **not supported**.

## Explicitly excluded from scope

State/local tax other than the Maryland subset above (every other state is rejected), AMT, NIIT on passive rents, Form 8960 investment deductions and foreign MAGI adjustments, short-term lots, wash sales, return of capital, §199A REIT dividends, foreign tax credit, exempt-interest dividends, capital loss carryovers under the legacy no-loss-offset label, employer match, qualified charitable distributions, pension benefit formulas, itemized deductions, most tax credits, substantial AGI/MAGI exceptions, Medicare IRMAA and health subsidies, filing-status changes, inheritance and survivor rules, Roth five-year test, 401k still-working exceptions, SSA earnings test and actuarial longevity. Such a user case must be escalated to expert judgment or the model expanded and independently verified **before** calculation.
